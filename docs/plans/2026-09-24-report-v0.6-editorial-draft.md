# CUA-RSIBench: Executable Data Research for Verifiable Computer Use

**Editorial draft for v0.6. Evidence cutoff: completed v0.5 studies. No new 4 x 6 experiment outcomes are included.**

Original cohort: **gpt-6-astra and gpt-5.6-sol**. Separate extension: **gpt-6-sol and gpt-6-luna**. Fixed teacher: **gpt-5.6-sol**. Trained student: **Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B**.

[Implementation, released evidence, and artifacts](https://github.com/EnvLoop/cua-rsibench/releases/tag/v0.5.0-executable-factories)

## Abstract

We evaluate researcher agents that write executable training-data factories for a separate browser student. Factories generate native Kanboard states, request independently verified demonstrations, and revise training mixtures using selection feedback. Each admitted candidate starts from the same Qwen3.5-4B base and receives real LoRA updates. Two completed cohorts each ran ten research rounds: the original cohort trained seven candidates using 832,655 scheduled tokens; the Sol 6 / Luna 6 extension trained six using 804,299.

In the extension, the student selected by gpt-6-sol achieved 16.7% final success, compared with 0.0% for the base. gpt-6-luna retained that base and shares its execution evidence. In the original cohort, the Astra-selected student's valid original mean was 25.0%; base and Sol 5.6 original means remain undefined because of infrastructure failures. Separate full-suite operational replays yielded 0.0% for base and 16.7% for the Sol 5.6-selected student. Controller platforms differ across those original-cohort roles. The cohorts also differ in final records and research-interface timing; they are not a matched four-model ranking. These results establish a bounded data-research pilot, not sustained recursive improvement or broad computer-use generalization.

## 1. Method and comparison boundaries

The data-research formulation follows [RSIBench-Data (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25886): vary the researcher's data strategy while fixing training and evaluation services.

The researcher writes and executes Python, constructs permitted task recipes, requests a fixed teacher's GUI rollouts, and submits a verified messages dataset. It can filter, deduplicate, repeat, reorder, and mix decision-only or full-history examples. Positive examples must reconstruct actual independently verified trajectories; invented observations and unexecuted successful demonstrations are rejected. Each valid dataset trains a fresh adapter from the same base. A previous adapter is never the starting point for the next candidate.

Promotion requires a strict mean gain on three frozen selection instances with no per-task regression. Ties retain the incumbent, including the base. All searches within a cohort close before final evaluation; final outcomes never become research feedback. Researcher IDs identify data-policy authors, not the model acting in the final browser trials.

| Cohort | Research interface and evidence boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Original: gpt-6-astra, gpt-5.6-sol | Explicit remaining-turn deadlines were added in round 5. Early rounds did not receive the extension's round-1 baseline diagnostics. |
| Extension: gpt-6-sol, gpt-6-luna | Fresh factory lineages begin with explicit deadlines and baseline diagnostics. No original-cohort programs, datasets, or trajectories are inherited. |

The cohorts share selection packages and the repaired base-selection execution. Its reuse is not a new run or independent sample. Each cohort has six final variants and two same-seed environment repetitions, using different final source records. The extension's final base and selected-student executions all use the Linux controller. Its selection baseline came from the Mac, while later candidate evaluations used Linux; selection deltas therefore do not isolate training-data effects from operational or observation variation.

## 2. Environment, data, and verification

Trials run the original [Kanboard 1.2.54 application](https://github.com/kanboard/kanboard/releases/tag/v1.2.54), including PHP forms, comments, projects, and SQLite persistence. Every episode starts from fresh application state. Setup operations receive no agent credit. The actor reads visible DOM text and control references and can click, fill, select, use limited keypresses, go back, and finish. It has no shell, SQL, application API, file editor, or arbitrary-URL action. Screenshots are audit artifacts, not model inputs.

The source snapshot contains 37 authentic public issue metadata records: URLs, numbers, capped titles, states, timestamps, labels, and comment counts. Training, selection, and final pools contain twelve nonoverlapping IDs each; one record is unused. Bodies, authors, and email addresses are excluded. Staff, rules, planning values, costs, capacities, dependencies, project names, and archive distractors are explicitly synthetic.

| Cohort | Selection IDs used | Final IDs used | Training-supervision IDs used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original | 5 | 4 | 9 |
| Extension | 5 | 4 | 8 |

The task families are direct record updates, conditional ranking, and constrained allocation. Final variants change record order, target attributes, ranking direction, and planning alternatives. This is one application with shared templates; the variants jointly probe robustness, not the causal effect of an isolated difficulty factor.

A separate Harbor verifier compares initial and final database snapshots. Strict success requires every intended edit and preservation of unrelated records and business tables. Completion claims earn no reward. Only native timestamps, null/zero time fields, and newline equivalences are normalized; missing records, wrong targets, changed comments, and extra edits fail.

Generated programs run in a separate E2B research sandbox under a non-root account, empty network namespace, no-new-privileges, and resource limits. Inputs are read-only; provider credentials, host files, private journals, final tasks, and hidden answers are excluded. Live isolation checks verified these boundaries and cleanup. Training and evaluation share a prompt constructor, and token preflight rejects oversized sequences, targets, or schedules that omit submitted records.

## 3. Fixed services and execution accounting

Researchers use AgentRouterHub's Responses interface. Exact model IDs are provider request identifiers, not independent attestations of hosted weights. [Tinker](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker) performs actual LoRA updates and checkpoint sampling; an authenticated [E2B](https://github.com/e2b-dev/E2B) proxy serves the checkpoint; [Harbor](https://github.com/laude-institute/harbor) runs the tasks and separate verifiers.

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Research allowance | At most 5 rounds and 1,048,576 scheduled training tokens per researcher |
| Per-round generation | 20 logical turns; up to 40 charged calls with retries; 12 program runs; 3 new teacher rollouts; 100 teacher calls |
| Training | 32 updates; batch 2; LoRA rank 8; learning rate 0.0001; seed 23 |
| Data exposure | At most 64 fully covered records; 16,384 tokens per sequence; 262,144 scheduled tokens per candidate |
| Student sampling | Temperature 0; seed 23; maximum 512 output tokens |
| Task execution | Maximum 90 actions; 1,500-second actor timeout; 3 tasks per execution chunk |
| Finals | 6 variants per cohort; 2 fresh-environment repetitions at the same seed |

Scheduled tokens include masked inputs and are not a dollar estimate. Provider dollar totals are unknown. Rejected submissions and infrastructure-invalid executions are unscored, never zero. Training requires a reservation; uncertain paid failures remain conservatively charged. Original-cohort rounds 1 and 2 preceded this guard and are identified as historical imports in the ledger.

The original serving cache held 256 entries, insufficient for three 90-action trials. It was repaired to 270, and the paired base and initial candidates were rerun with unchanged weights and task packages. A separately versioned controller amendment later moved orchestration from Mac to Linux E2B, checking 98 dependency versions and nine installed source-file hashes. Python patch versions differ: 3.12.13 on the host and 3.12.14 remotely. Frozen actor, task, verifier, checkpoint, and sampling bindings, plus downloaded archive/member hashes, are checked before admission.

Infrastructure-invalid original final slots receive a separately reported full six-task replay. Each recovered mean uses six fresh rows, without mixing in original successes. Valid originals are not replayed. These remain the same logical repetitions, not additional independent samples or research seeds.

## 5. Interpretation and limitations

The executed loop includes researcher-authored programs, new native task states, verified GUI demonstrations, real weight updates, and independent final evaluation. It does not establish sustained recursive self-improvement: the smaller student never becomes the next researcher. More verified data also did not reliably improve selection performance. Appendix A records the retained and last trained datasets without treating them as controlled representation ablations.

Each cohort has one application, three shared task families, one research seed per system, three selection instances, and six final variants. Repeated environments at the same seed test execution stability, not independent task-distribution draws. They support neither general confidence intervals nor a frontier-model ranking. Records, templates, UI structure, and workflow conventions recur, and public metadata may already appear in pretraining. Source separation does not establish unseen-template or unseen-application generalization.

Nuisance state can alter trajectories. In Luna 6 round 2, the original direct task passed and the declared remote recovery failed despite identical first-prompt and first-response hashes on all three tasks. At step 3, removing CSRF query values and displayed creation/modification/move times made the compared observations identical; subsequent actions diverged. This identifies a reproducibility limitation, not a causal explanation. The study did not normalize those observations or exclude inference variability.

Interface compliance also matters. In Luna 6 round 5, 11 replies added prose before an otherwise valid allowed JSON action. The fixed parser accepts clean fenced JSON but does not strip prose. All 20 provider receipts reported completion; rejected replies used 112-191 output tokens. The round ended without submission, training, or a task score. This was not output-token exhaustion; raw provider envelopes are unavailable, so the contribution of response aggregation remains unknown.

Larger final suites, multiple research seeds, an equal-budget non-adaptive search control, and calibrated difficulty tiers are needed for stronger attribution. Controller-enforced no-regression promotion is narrower than unrestricted checkpoint choice. Earlier direct-actor calibration establishes limited workflow solvability, not student calibration on every instance. No pixel-only grounding, original Microsoft Office, full Windows/macOS operation, cross-application enterprise capability, or production-scale backlog realism is measured.

## 6. Reproducibility and next study

The release preserves separate cohort evidence, original failures, operational amendments, factory programs and hashes, verifier outcomes, manuscript, figures, and an interactive explorer. Audits reconstruct training provenance and bind datasets, checkpoints, task packages, source/runtime versions, resource use, promotion, and final-test timing. Credentials, private checkpoint identifiers, raw provider envelopes, and host paths are excluded. The historical v0.4 selection-only pilot remains separate.

The requested full-scale 4 x 6 work is a separate pre-results study using Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B as its student. Its authorization does not establish execution or performance, and it contributes no outcomes, rankings, or resource totals to the completed Qwen3.5-4B results above. New results require their own frozen design, completed runs, and audited evidence before inclusion.
