# GitLab CE v0.6.6: prospective recovery after the terminal fourth batch

The current source-frozen GitLab continuation is terminal. Its first 13 original-roster identities have independent GUI, persisted PostgreSQL/Git, score, and reset audits. The next identity failed while creating a fresh disposable world after a partial positive GUI action; the child exited with a readiness `TimeoutError`, the supervisor recorded no replay, and an exact healthy baseline was independently restored. The disposable container exited with code 1 during GitLab Omnibus nginx log-service startup. The underlying cause remains unknown because the failed container's raw log was not retained. An earlier original attempt stopped at a different runit log service. A longer health wait would not cure a container that has already exited.

There are three possible successor paths. Replaying the failed identity would blur the terminal record and is prohibited for this epoch. Jumping to the next original identity would stay inside the failed identity's correlated five-task source family; it cannot replace the failed task or preserve an independent 100-task denominator. This plan therefore separates an optional **cross-epoch diagnostic** at the earliest unused identity in another original family from a **prospective replacement cohort**. A diagnostic result would test infrastructure and GUI behavior only; it cannot be counted as the fourteenth final control or an official model outcome.

For a publishable 100-task GitLab cohort, retire the complete original family at indices 10–14. Three previously passing controls in that family remain immutable historical evidence and do not transfer into the replacement cohort. Reconstruct the first five-task FIFO reserve family from the preserved private world seed, original world, and pinned source catalog, and require its digest to match the pre-result public commitment. The proposed private roster has the original indices 0–9, five reserve identities, and original indices 15–99: 20 complete, disjoint five-task source families and 100 distinct identities. The existing ten retained controls are supporting evidence under the old plan, not admissions under a new plan hash or baseline. The reserve is inventory only and its project has not been bootstrapped into the current disposable world.

The offline `gitlab_world.v066_infra_recovery_v4` source freezes the terminal journal and independent audit, original plan, reconstructed FIFO commitment, candidate roster, and a new epoch digest. Its public plan contains counts and hashes only; task IDs, prompts, answer state, and reserve source records remain private. It has **no Docker or provider action and no task-dispatch command**. Both the diagnostic and replacement intent flags are false. Any source drift, changed terminal history, overlapping reserve identity, partial-family swap, missing private input, or later old-branch intent fails the plan audit.

A safe next live diagnostic requires another reviewed source freeze with a distinct one-ID intent, process-group supervision, and no replay. Before that intent, perform source-bound boot-only clones against copied seed storage with exact baseline readback, record phase times and host/VM memory, disk, inode, and I/O observations, and preserve Docker inspect state, Docker/Omnibus logs, runit logger status, and VM diagnostics **before** cleanup on failure. The later one-ID diagnostic must save the positive/wrong/repeated-positive GUI trio, independent persisted-state scoring, unrelated-change rejection, and three exact cold resets; a new failure stays terminal in that new epoch.

The replacement cohort additionally requires bootstrapping the FIFO reserve project into a resettable original GitLab CE world, freezing a new baseline and ACL proof, creating source-bound packages and a new 100-identity plan, validating source separation, and independently auditing every identity. No result in this file authorizes live dispatch, official final-task admission, a researcher campaign, or a model score.
