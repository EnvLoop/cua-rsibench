# Independent source review of four SEC TRAIN workbook cases

**Finding: all 64 used 2024/2025 field mappings and presence conventions match the frozen filing evidence, but 32 pension context-scope metadata entries are wrong.** These mappings include 58 numerical values and six facts not separately reported; one numerical source fact is a disclosed dash. The [read-only audit receipt](sec-next-four-second-person-source-audit-2026-09-30.json) binds a private field-by-field record by SHA-256. It contains no issuer IDs, accession numbers, task answers, or account data. This audit did not edit the authored review or workbooks and did not run Excel for the web, a model, or an official final task.

The [independent parser](../../tools/sec_next_four_second_person_audit_v1.py) reopened the frozen original 2025 10-K, same-accession indexed supporting document where applicable, SEC submissions response, and CompanyFacts bytes for each of four distinct TRAIN issuers. It rediscovered the filing tables by headings and XBRL concepts rather than using the author's table indices. For the two pension filings, it selected the inline XBRL facts by full-year/instant period and plan dimensions, including the U.S.-plan dimension where present. It checked signed benefit outflows, disclosed dashes versus facts not separately reported, the plan-asset rollforward, funded status, and the cross-year asset carry. Scoped pension facts were **not** described as CompanyFacts-verified: the same-accession CompanyFacts response does not expose those dimensioned plan facts.

For the two bank filings, it verified 20 tagged full-year interest and year-end deposit values against **the same issuer and accession** in CompanyFacts, keeping annual-average interest-bearing deposits in their separate, untagged filing table. It checked that interest income less interest expense equals reported net interest income, that annual averages are not substituted for closing balances, and that the supporting document is indexed under the 10-K accession. All bank context scopes and all 64 field values, missing-data states, row hashes, tags, context references, and 2024/2025 periods agree with the authored review.

The scope defect is specific and reproducible. In the saved HTML DOM, XBRL dimension elements are named `xbrldi:explicitmember` in lowercase. The author's [`_period` extraction](../../tools/sec_train_next_four_review_v1.py) looks for case-sensitive `explicitMember`, so its `dimension_member_count` is zero even where the actual contexts contain dimensions. Fourteen checked aggregate-pension fields have **one** actual plan member, and eighteen checked U.S.-pension fields have **two** actual members (plan and U.S. geography). Both bank filings correctly have zero. This incorrect metadata does not change the numerical inputs or current offline workbook scorer: the builder and scorer use the matched values, presence flags, XBRL context references, and source rows, rather than `dimension_member_count`. It does weaken the published scope evidence until the author reruns a corrected extraction and the new review is independently checked.

The existing cohort statement that the source-field review “passed” should be qualified to **“numeric fields match; pension dimension metadata correction pending.”** Keep the current workbooks and old review as historical artifacts; issue an additive corrected private review and public receipt after fixing the XPath and rechecking source hashes. Original Excel-for-the-web save/readback/reset admission remains zero for these cases, and formal final admissions remain zero.
