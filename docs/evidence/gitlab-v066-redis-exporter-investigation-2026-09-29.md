# GitLab disposable-clone startup investigation

The failed current-profile evaluator control ended with a saved TimeoutError: the disposable GitLab world did not become healthy. Before the source-frozen supervisor removed the failed container, a read-only Docker log inspection showed GitLab Omnibus reconfiguration failing while reloading the redis-exporter runit log service. The command reported that the log service was down although it was normally expected to be up. Docker recorded exit code 1 and OOMKilled=false. The supervisor subsequently recreated a disposable container, verified it healthy, and read back the exact frozen business baseline without replaying the failed ID.

The [terminal capacity observation](gitlab-v066-batch-001-terminal-capacity-2026-09-29.json) found approximately 29 GiB available on the host Data volume and 13.3 GiB available in the GitLab VM Docker volume; Docker-volume inode use was 11%. A read-only VM kernel-journal search for OOM, no-space, and I/O errors in the incident window returned no matching lines. The recovered container's redis-exporter and its log supervisor were both running, and its Omnibus configuration completed. The failed container's raw Docker log bytes and digest were not captured before its mandated cleanup.

These observations locate the immediate failure in startup/reconfiguration and show that the inspected filesystems were not exhausted. They do not establish why the redis-exporter log service was down. A transient runit startup race is plausible but unproven; host storage pressure, scheduling contention, and other causes have not been independently excluded. The original failed attempt and the capacity limitation remain visible in the public evidence.
